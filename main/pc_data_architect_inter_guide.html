5. Interview Q&A

TOC
1Interview Q&A

Interview Q&A

Open each question for a practical answer aligned with your background.

1. What is your understanding of this role?

I understand this role as building the trusted data layer around the energy-trading lifecycle. The challenge is not only storing data, but integrating ETRM, market, operations and finance data, standardising business definitions, enforcing quality and lineage, and delivering reliable data products for trading, risk, settlement, accounting and management.

It is therefore a combination of data architecture, hands-on pipeline engineering, governance and stakeholder management.

2. Why are you interested in this role?

I am interested because the role combines complex business processes, financial control, and modern data architecture. Energy trading requires strong accuracy, timeliness, traceability, and reconciliation, which are closely related to my experience in cross-border payments and data governance.

I also like that the position focuses on building a reusable data foundation rather than only maintaining reports.

3. You do not have direct ETRM experience. Why should we hire you?

That is a genuine domain gap, and I would not claim otherwise. However, the core engineering problem is familiar to me: multiple operational systems, complex lifecycle states, strict financial accuracy, reconciliation, common business definitions and auditability.

In cross-border payments, I designed data models and pipelines covering institutions, orders, transactions, fund movements and regulatory reconciliation. I can bring that architecture and control discipline immediately, while learning the ETRM product and energy-market concepts systematically.

Key approach: acknowledge the gap, then explain the transferable problem structure.
4. How would you approach a messy data environment?

I would begin with discovery rather than immediately selecting a platform. First, I would inventory source systems, reports, interfaces, owners and critical data elements. Second, I would profile the data and identify duplicates, conflicting definitions, missing controls and manual reconciliation points.

I would then prioritise one high-value business flow, define canonical entities and quality rules, build lineage and ownership, and deliver an initial governed data product. The architecture would be scaled incrementally rather than through a large one-time migration.

5. How would you design the target architecture?

I would separate the architecture into source, ingestion, storage, transformation, governance and consumption layers. ETRM, market feeds, logistics and finance systems would enter through batch, CDC, APIs or selective streaming based on latency requirements.

The platform would retain immutable source-aligned data, create canonical trade and reference entities, and publish curated products for risk, operations and finance. Quality, lineage, security, observability and reconciliation would be cross-cutting controls.

6. Star Schema or Data Vault?

I would not treat them as mutually exclusive. Data Vault can be useful in the integration layer when sources change frequently and full historical traceability is important. Star Schema is usually more suitable for business-facing risk, finance and management analytics because it is easier to understand and query.

A practical pattern is source-aligned raw data, a historically traceable canonical core, and dimensional data marts for consumption.

7. How do you ensure data quality in a trading environment?

I would define quality rules around critical data elements and business events, not only technical null checks. Examples include unique trade identifiers, valid lifecycle transitions, quantity and currency consistency, market-price freshness, counterparty completeness and reconciliation between trade, settlement and accounting records.

Controls should include preventive validation, pipeline quality gates, exception tables, ownership, SLA monitoring and audit-ready evidence.

8. How do you decide between batch and streaming?

I start from the business decision latency. Daily regulatory or finance reporting may be batch-based, while market updates, intraday positions or limit monitoring may require event-driven or near-real-time processing.

I would avoid streaming everything. Streaming adds operational complexity, so it should be used where lower latency creates clear business or control value.

9. Tell us about a relevant reconciliation project.

In my cross-border payments work, I built a five-table regulatory reconciliation chain covering a 30-day window. The solution standardised business keys, used window functions to remove duplicates, compared records across lifecycle stages and produced exception tables for investigation.

The relevant lesson for trading is that reconciliation must be designed into the data model and pipeline, rather than added as a final reporting check.

10. Tell us about a performance optimisation.

I investigated a Spark aggregation job that suffered from data skew and memory pressure. I redesigned it using two-stage aggregation, salting and adaptive query execution, and changed the aggregation strategy where appropriate. Runtime improved from approximately 75 minutes to 13 minutes.

This experience shows how I combine execution-plan analysis, data-distribution understanding and practical engineering changes.
